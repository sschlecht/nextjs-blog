A purple cow is something that wows and shocks someone because it is so different than the norm.

A great example of a purple cow, in 2025, somewhat unknown Truett Hanes passed a professional marathoner, Janet Rono, and finished in 2:42:51. He did it all the while running in jeans and with a chisled body better suited to be in a body building a gym. Unsurprisingly, his story went viral.

A Purple Cow was popularized by Seth Godin to describe something that naturally wows and shocks people enough to naturally share and talk about it. It’s wildly different than a field of brown cows you may drive by.

The same principle can apply in marketing and getting your message across to your audience.  In this post, I’ll share examples how you can stand out in a sea of sameness.

## What Makes a good Purple Cow in Marketing?

Purple cows in marketing come in all shapes and flavors. In my marketing experience, a few criteria that makes a good purple cow are:

1. **Pattern interruption by design:** It must go against the grain. The brain looks for patterns and interrupting those patterns makes one pause to process what they are seeing.
2. **It is worth talking about:** Most of our days are filled with "sameness." A Purple Cow is with talking about with family, friends, and strangers. It usually conjures shock, awe, and critism among other emotions.
3. **It targets a specific group:** A good purple cow resonates with a group of people or a particular interest group. It follows the Reddit acronym of "IYKYK" (If you know, you know).

- So how can you use the Purple Cow principle in you marketing strategy? I'll share a few examples how you can stand out and increase your return on marketing dollars.

## How to Build a Purple Cow by Channel

### 1. Direct Mail

- **The Brown Cow (Default):** A standard flyer about a commoditized service: HVAC repair, car insurance, etc.
- **The Purple Cow (Remarkable):** Nielsen had a famous marketing direct mail campaign that put two $1 bills in the mailer to encourage individuals to respond. Who gets $2 in the mail for free? No one. The campaign ended up being highly successful.
- **Purple Cow Ideas:** Depending on your budget, a mailer that expands into a massive 5' x 5', a mailer that has a wax seal on it, or an intentional stain.

### 2. Content & Thought Leadership

- **The Brown Cow (Default):** You know the guy or gal that sounds off the same career advice or promoting their company. There's tons of them.
- **The Purple Cow (Remarkable):** My friend Reed Loustalot ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/reedloustalot/)) is in the freight industry and a great example of a purple cow. His profile picture is him with a cigar and a hat saying "Please Advise." Nothing about that is the standard professional picture you see on LinkedIn. It breaks the pattern.
- **Purple Cow Ideas:** Why not post something on LinkedIn while hanging upside down? Or with your shirt off? It often seems crazy in the moment, but it stands out.

### 3. Email & Outbound

- **The Brown Cow (Default):** I used to be a mass email marketer and been on the receiving end of the "Quick question" subject lines. Or promotional emails that have the same 10-20% discount for you to come back to their store.
- **The Purple Cow (Remarkable):** I have a condo in Chicago. I got a promotional email recently that said "Home prices go up by X% in Chicago." It broke the pattern of generic subject lines, spoke directly to me, and encourage me to talk about it with my partner about how my home's value has gone up.
- **Purple Cow Ideas:** How can you create an email campaign that speaks to 1 person at scale, creates a sense of surprise, and encourages people to talk about it? My friend owns a reef safe sunscreen company and recently had a campaign that had a subject line calling out the hormone disruption in women from traditional sunscreens. It worked. It created a sense of shock, was atypical, and made other women talk about it with one another.

## The 3-Question Purple Cow Litmus Test

1. **Remarkability:** Is it remarkable and interupt the psyche?
2. **Sharability:** Does it make the person want to talk about it?
3. **Lasting:** Does it last and turn heads once the novelty wears off?

## Conclusion: The Biggest Risk Is Playing It Safe

In order to stand out in modern marketing, you have to do something different. A great way to stand out is using the principles of the Purple Cow in your marketing strategy.
